# How to Tilt a Window AC

A window air conditioner pulls moisture out of the room air as it cools, and that water has to go somewhere. For most units, the answer is a slight slope toward the outside, so condensate runs out the back instead of into your window sill.

**Most window air conditioners should slope slightly down toward the outside, by the amount the installation manual specifies, so condensate drains outdoors. Check it with a level across the top of the unit, front to back. Some units are designed to sit level; the manual is the authority on which yours is.**

## Why tilt matters

Water collects in a pan at the bottom of the unit. With the right slope, the excess drains out the back. With no slope, or a slope toward the room, it can overflow inside, staining the sill and wall and feeding mold.

## Why it shouldn't tilt too much

Many units are designed to keep some water in the base pan: a ring on the condenser fan flings it onto the hot coil, where it evaporates and helps cool the coil. Too steep a slope drains that water away, and the unit can also sit less securely in the window. So the right amount is the manual's, not "as much as possible".

## How to set and check the tilt

1. Install the unit and its support bracket as the instructions direct.
2. Rest a torpedo level across the top of the unit, pointing front to back. A torpedo level is short enough to sit on the cabinet without overhanging the ends, so it reads the unit's own slope rather than the sill's.
3. Adjust the bracket or the support until the bubble shows a slight slope toward the outside, by the amount the manual calls for.
4. Confirm the magnitude, not just the direction. Measure the height of the front lip and the back lip above the sill with a ruler or tape, then compare the difference to the figure in your manual.
5. Recheck both the bubble and the measured drop after you tighten the brackets and close the sash. Hardware and sashes shift as they are torqued, so a slope that was right loose can change once everything is snug.

## Units designed to sit level

Not every window unit wants a slope. U-shaped and saddle units straddle the window sash, with the compressor section hanging outside and the quieter section inside and a gap where the window closes between them. These are built to sit level and route condensate through their outdoor half.

Many newer units are self-evaporating. A slinger ring on the condenser fan lifts water from the base pan onto the hot condenser coil, where the heat of operation evaporates it and sends it out with the exhaust air. These are designed to hold water in the pan rather than drain it, so a steep tilt that empties the pan works against the design.

To tell which type you own, check three things in order. The rating label and the installation manual usually state whether the unit mounts level or at a slope, and that text is the authority. The body shape is the quick visual cue: a boxy cabinet that projects straight out the back is the traditional sloped type, while a unit that notches around the sash is a U-shaped or saddle design.

## Drainage problems and what they mean

Most drainage complaints trace back to one of four symptoms. The table maps each to its usual cause and the fix, so you can tell a real leak from normal operation.

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Water dripping inside | Unit slopes toward the room, sits level when it should slope, or the rear drain path is blocked | Recheck the slope with a level, then clear the rear drain channel and weep holes |
| Gurgling or sloshing | The slinger ring is picking up water from the base pan | Normal on self-evaporating units; no action needed |
| No drip outside on a humid day | A self-evaporating unit is evaporating its condensate, or the drain path is blocked | Normal if the room feels dry; if the room stays humid, check the slope and clear the drain path |
| Ice on the indoor coil | Low airflow, usually from a dirty filter, not from the tilt | Clean or replace the filter and clean the coil; see [how to clean AC coils](/how-to-clean-ac-coils) |

A unit that still leaks inside after the slope and drain-path checks is usually fighting a frozen coil that melts when it cycles off, or a drain overwhelmed by very humid air. The [AC troubleshooting guide](/ac-troubleshooting-guide) walks through that wider set of causes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a window AC tilt back?

Most should slope slightly toward the outside so condensate drains out the back. Some are designed to sit level; check the manual.

### How much should a window air conditioner tilt?

By the amount the installation manual specifies; it varies by model. Check it with a level, front to back.

### Why is my window AC leaking water inside?

Usually because it slopes toward the room or sits level when it shouldn't, or the drain path is clogged.

### Should I drill a hole in my window AC to drain it?

No. Many units are designed to keep some water in the pan to cool the coil; drilling can reduce efficiency and may void the warranty.

### Can a window AC be installed perfectly level?

Only if the manual says so. U-shaped, saddle, and many self-evaporating units are built to sit level and manage water internally. A traditional unit that calls for a slope will leak indoors if you set it dead level, so follow the label and manual for your model.

### My window AC is leaking inside but it is tilted correctly, what else could it be?

Check the rear drain channel and weep holes for debris, pollen, or insect nests that stop water from leaving. A dirty coil or a blocked drain lets the pan overflow even at the right slope. A coil that freezes and then melts when the unit cycles off can also drip inside, which usually traces back to a dirty filter or low airflow.

### Does tilting a window AC too much damage it?

Too steep a slope drains the water that self-evaporating units rely on to cool the condenser coil, which can lower efficiency. It can also leave the unit sitting less securely in the window. The correct amount is the manual's specified slope, not as much tilt as the window allows.
