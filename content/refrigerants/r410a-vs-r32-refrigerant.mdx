# R-410A vs R-32 vs R-454B

For two decades, nearly every new home air conditioner and heat pump in the U.S. used R-410A. Under the EPA's phase-down of high-global-warming refrigerants, new systems now use R-454B or R-32 instead, and both are classed as mildly flammable, which changes how equipment is built and serviced.

**R-410A has a global warming potential (GWP) of 2,088; R-32 is 675 and R-454B is 466, cuts of about 68% and 78%. Both replacements are A2L refrigerants, lower toxicity and lower flammability, so new equipment carries safety features R-410A systems didn't need. Existing R-410A systems can still be repaired.**

## How they compare

| | R-410A | R-32 | R-454B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global warming potential | 2,088 | 675 | 466 |
| Safety class | A1 (non-flammable) | A2L (mildly flammable) | A2L (mildly flammable) |
| Composition | Blend | Single compound | Blend of R-32 and R-1234yf |
| Typical use now | Existing systems | Many mini splits and some ducted systems | Many ducted systems |

R-454B is about 69% R-32 and 31% R-1234yf by weight. Manufacturers have split between the two replacements for new equipment; the refrigerant is listed on the outdoor unit's nameplate and the spec sheet.

## Why the change

The EPA's Technology Transitions rule limits new residential air conditioning and heat pump systems to refrigerants with a GWP below 700, starting with equipment manufactured from 2025. R-410A, at 2,088, is over the limit; R-32 and R-454B are under it. See the [refrigerant phase-out](/hvac-refrigerant-phase-out) for the dates.

## What A2L means

ASHRAE's Standard 34 classifies refrigerants by toxicity (A = lower) and flammability (1 = none, 2L = lower flammability, 2 and 3 = higher). A2L refrigerants are hard to ignite and burn slowly, but they can burn, so equipment is designed and tested to the UL 60335-2-40 safety standard, which can require leak detection and ventilation for larger refrigerant charges. Technicians need A2L-rated tools and training.

## What it means for you

- **An existing R-410A system** can keep running and be repaired with R-410A, which remains available for servicing.
- **A new system** will use R-32 or R-454B; you can't mix refrigerants or add an A2L refrigerant to an R-410A system.
- **Replacing only the outdoor unit** of an R-410A system with a new-refrigerant unit isn't possible; the indoor coil has to be replaced to match, and the line set may need to be too.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is R-32 better than R-454B?

Neither is clearly better. R-32 is a single compound with a GWP of 675; R-454B has a lower GWP of 466. Both are A2L, and both meet the EPA limit.

### Can I still buy an R-410A air conditioner?

New R-410A systems are no longer being manufactured for the U.S. residential market; see the phase-out for the installation dates. R-410A remains available to service existing systems.

### Are A2L refrigerants dangerous?

They're mildly flammable, which is why equipment using them must meet the UL 60335-2-40 safety standard. In normal operation they're sealed inside the system.

### Can I put R-32 in my R-410A system?

No. Systems are designed for one refrigerant; mixing or substituting can damage the equipment and violate safety standards.
