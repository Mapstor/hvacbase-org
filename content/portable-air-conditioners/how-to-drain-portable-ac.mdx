# How to Drain a Portable AC

A portable air conditioner pulls moisture out of the room air as it cools. Many models get rid of most of that water by evaporating it into the exhaust air, but in humid weather the water collects faster than it evaporates, and the unit shuts itself off when its internal tank is full.

**To drain it, unplug the unit, place a shallow pan under the drain port at the back near the floor, remove the plug and let it empty, then replace the plug. If it fills often, connect a hose to the drain port for continuous gravity drainage to a floor drain, or use a condensate pump to lift the water out.**

## Why some portables rarely need draining

Many portables use the collected water to cool the condenser coil and send it out the exhaust hose as vapor. In dry weather, that keeps up with the water collected, and the unit may almost never need draining. In humid weather, or in dehumidify mode, it can't keep up, so the tank fills.

## The three drain designs, and how to tell which you have

Portable units handle condensate in one of three ways, and the design decides how often you touch it.

- **Self-evaporating ("no-drain"):** most condensate lands on the hot condenser coil and leaves through the exhaust hose as vapor. In dry air the unit may almost never fill; in humid air or dehumidify mode the water it cannot evaporate still collects in a tank.
- **Internal-tank (manual drain):** condensate pools in a reservoir and the unit shuts off when the float reads full. You empty it by hand through the lower port.
- **Continuous-drain:** a threaded or barbed port stays open to a hose, so water runs off as fast as it forms and never pools.

Most portables are a blend of these: self-evaporating while cooling, with a tank and a drain port for humid spells and for dehumidify mode.

To tell which you own, look at the back panel and the manual. A single low port near the floor is the tank drain; a second port higher up is the continuous-drain outlet, usually used in dehumidify mode. A unit with no visible port and only a full-tank light is mostly self-evaporating with an internal tank.

## Draining by hand

1. **Turn the unit off and unplug it.**
2. **Move it** to a spot where a little spilled water won't matter, or put a towel down.
3. **Place a shallow pan** under the lower drain port; portables sit low, so a baking sheet or shallow tray works better than a bucket.
4. **Remove the drain plug** and let the water drain; tilt the unit slightly toward the port to finish.
5. **Replace the plug firmly** before turning it back on.

Check the manual: some units have two ports, one for the condensate tank and one for continuous drainage in dehumidify mode.

## Continuous drainage

For humid weather, a hose on the drain port lets water run out continuously. It only works by gravity, so the hose has to run downhill all the way to a floor drain, a sink below the port's height, or outdoors, with no kinks or upward loops.

## Using a condensate pump

If there's no drain lower than the port, a small condensate pump collects the water and pumps it up and out to a sink or window. It needs an outlet, and its tubing must be secured where it discharges.

## Upper and lower drain ports

Many portables have two drain points at different heights, and each does a different job.

The lower port sits at the bottom of the reservoir near the floor. It empties the tank completely, so it is the one to use for manual draining and for end-of-season storage.

The upper port sits higher on the back, at the level where dehumidify mode collects water. A hose here carries water off during operation, but the pan below it still holds a little, so the upper port never leaves the unit bone dry.

Because the upper port is higher, a gravity hose on it begins draining sooner; the lower port drains to empty but only when you open it. Use the upper port for a continuous hose while the unit runs, and the lower port to empty it for cleaning or storage.

## Choosing a drain method

| Method | Added equipment | Effort | Best for | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manual pan drain | None | Recurring, by hand | Dry climates and light use, when the tank fills only now and then | You have to be there when it fills |
| Continuous gravity hose | A drain hose | Set up once | Humid weather and long runs, when a drain sits below the port | Needs a downhill path and cannot lift water |
| Condensate pump | A pump and tubing | Set up once, needs power | When no drain sits lower than the unit, or water must go up to a sink or window | Needs an outlet and adds one more part that can fail |

## How often to drain

It depends on humidity, room size and how long the unit runs. In humid weather a portable can fill in hours; in dry weather, rarely at all. The full-tank indicator and automatic shutoff tell you when.

## Preventing leaks and mold

- **Keep the drain plug tight** and the unit level.
- **Drain it fully before storing it**, then run the fan for a while or let it air dry with the plug out; standing water grows mold, which the EPA advises controlling by keeping things dry.
- **Don't drink or reuse the condensate**; it passes through the unit's coils and pan.
- **Clean the filter** regularly; a dirty filter can make the coil freeze and then drip as it thaws.

## Draining and drying for winter storage

At the end of cooling season, clear all water out before the unit sits for months, or standing condensate can grow mold and leave a musty smell.

1. **Unplug the unit** and open the lower drain port over a shallow pan to empty the reservoir; tilt the unit toward the port to clear the last of it.
2. **Run fan-only mode** until the coils and inside are dry, or leave the plug out and let it air-dry.
3. **Remove and wash the filter**, let it dry fully, then refit it.
4. **Coil the hoses, leave the drain plug out or loose**, and store the unit upright in a dry place.

Storing it upright keeps the compressor oil settled where it belongs, so the unit is ready to run next season.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I drain my portable AC?

Whenever the full-tank light comes on. In humid weather that may be every few hours; in dry weather it may rarely happen.

### Why does my portable AC fill up with water so fast?

High humidity, dehumidify mode, or a unit that doesn't evaporate its condensate. Continuous drainage solves it.

### Can I run a portable AC without draining it?

If it evaporates its condensate and the air is dry enough, often yes. Otherwise it will stop when the tank fills.

### Why is my portable AC leaking water?

Usually a loose drain plug, an uneven floor, a kinked drain hose, or a frozen coil from a dirty filter thawing.

### Where is the drain plug on my portable AC?

On most units it is a small capped or threaded port low on the back, near the floor, at the base of the condensate tank. Some models add a second port higher up for continuous draining in dehumidify mode. Check the back panel and the manual, since placement varies by model.

### Do all portable ACs need draining?

No. Self-evaporating models send most condensate out the exhaust as vapor and may rarely fill in dry weather. In humid weather or dehumidify mode, almost any portable will eventually collect water and need draining or a continuous hose.

### How do I drain it for winter storage?

Unplug the unit, open the lower port over a pan, and tilt it to empty the tank fully. Then run fan-only mode or leave the plug out until the coils and pan are dry, wash and dry the filter, and store it upright with the plug out.

### Can I leave a drain hose connected permanently?

Yes, if it drains by gravity to a lower point with no kinks or upward loops, a hose can stay connected for continuous draining. Use the upper port for this while the unit runs, and keep the discharge end clear. For storage, still open the lower port to empty the tank fully.
