# How Does Humidity Affect Temperature?

Humidity doesn't change what a thermometer reads, but it changes how warm you feel. Your body cools itself by evaporating sweat, and the more moisture the air already holds, the slower that evaporation goes.

**Humid air feels hotter because sweat evaporates more slowly. The National Weather Service's heat index puts 90°F at 70% humidity at a felt 106°F, and 100°F at 50% at 118°F. Indoors, the EPA recommends keeping humidity between 30% and 50%, and below 60%.**

## Why humid air feels hotter

Evaporating sweat carries heat away from the skin. When the air is already close to saturated, sweat evaporates slowly and the body sheds less heat, so the same temperature feels hotter. In dry air, sweat evaporates quickly, which is why dry heat feels more bearable.

## The heat index

The NWS heat index combines temperature and relative humidity into an apparent temperature, the temperature it feels like in the shade. Computed with the NWS's heat index equation:

| Air temperature | 40% humidity | 50% | 60% | 70% | 80% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 80°F | 80°F | 81°F | 82°F | 83°F | 84°F |
| 85°F | 84°F | 86°F | 89°F | 93°F | 97°F |
| 90°F | 91°F | 95°F | 100°F | 106°F | 113°F |
| 95°F | 99°F | 105°F | 113°F | 123°F | 134°F |
| 100°F | 109°F | 118°F | 129°F | 143°F | 158°F |

The heat index is defined for hot weather, about 80°F and above, and for shade; direct sun can add more. High values mean real risk of heat illness, which is why the NWS issues heat advisories from it.

## Dew point

Relative humidity depends on temperature: the same moisture reads as a higher percentage when the air cools. Dew point measures the moisture itself, the temperature at which the air would become saturated. When outdoor dew points climb into the upper 60s and 70s °F, the air feels muggy, whatever the relative humidity.

## Humidity indoors in summer

An air conditioner removes moisture as it cools, because water condenses on its cold indoor coil. If it's oversized and short-cycles, or if the weather is mild but humid, it may cool without drying the air enough, leaving the house cool but clammy. A dehumidifier or a properly sized system fixes that; see [running a dehumidifier and AC together](/dehumidifier-and-ac-same-time).

## Humidity indoors in winter

Cold outdoor air holds little moisture, so heated indoor air gets very dry, which can feel cooler and irritate skin and sinuses. ENERGY STAR suggests 30% to 40% indoor humidity in cold climates during the heating season, enough for comfort without condensation on windows.

## The range to aim for

The EPA recommends 30% to 50% indoors, and below 60% to limit mold and dust mites. See the [ideal indoor humidity level](/ideal-indoor-humidity-level).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does humidity make it feel hotter?

Yes. At 90°F, the heat index rises from about 91°F at 40% humidity to 106°F at 70%, according to the NWS heat index equation.

### Why does dry heat feel cooler?

Sweat evaporates quickly in dry air, carrying heat away from your skin efficiently.

### What humidity is comfortable indoors?

Between 30% and 50%, according to the EPA; in cold climates during winter, 30% to 40% avoids window condensation.

### Does lowering humidity make a room feel cooler?

Yes, at the same temperature. Drier air lets sweat evaporate more easily, which is why dehumidifying can let you set the thermostat a little higher in summer.
