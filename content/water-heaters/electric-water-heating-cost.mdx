# Electric Water Heating Cost and kWh Usage by State

Water heating is one of the largest electric loads in a home without gas, and its cost moves with two things you can see: how much hot water you use and what you pay per kWh. The calculator below turns your household and state into a monthly cost.

**A 50-gallon electric tank heating water for three people uses about 12.8 kWh a day, roughly 389 kWh a month: about $70 a month at 18 cents per kWh. A heat pump water heater does the same job for about $19.** Where electricity costs more, the gap grows.

## How the calculator estimates it

- **Hot water:** 20 gallons per person per day, times 0.75 for light use or 1.5 for heavy use.
- **Heat:** gallons × 8.34 pounds per gallon × a 70°F temperature rise, converted to kWh at 3,412 BTU per kWh.
- **Efficiency:** 0.90 for electric resistance tanks, 0.98 for electric tankless, and 3.5 for a heat pump water heater.
- **Standby loss:** 1.2, 1.4 and 1.8 kWh a day for 40-, 50- and 80-gallon tanks, 0.5 for a heat pump tank, none for tankless.
- **Rates:** pick your state to use its EIA average for January to July 2026, or enter the rate from your bill.

These are the calculator's assumptions for a typical household; your usage and your heater's rating decide the real number.

## By heater type

For three people at 18 cents per kWh: a 40-gallon tank costs about $68.98 a month, a 50-gallon $70.08, an 80-gallon $72.27, an electric tankless heater $57.32, and a heat pump water heater $18.79. Bigger tanks cost a little more mainly because they lose more heat standing by. The heat pump's advantage comes from moving heat out of the surrounding air rather than making it from electricity.

## How many kWh a water heater uses

The calculator's model gives the daily and yearly electricity for the same three-person household, about 60 gallons of hot water a day at average use, by heater type:

| Heater | kWh per day | kWh per year |
|---|---|---|
| 40-gallon tank | 12.6 | 4,600 |
| 50-gallon tank | 12.8 | 4,675 |
| 80-gallon tank | 13.2 | 4,820 |
| Electric tankless | 10.5 | 3,825 |
| Heat pump water heater | 3.4 | 1,255 |

Every resistance tank spends about 11.4 kWh a day heating the water itself; the rest is standby loss, the heat the tank radiates while sitting full. A bigger tank holds more hot water and has more surface, so it loses more: 1.2 kWh a day for a 40-gallon against 1.8 for an 80-gallon. That 0.6 kWh a day gap is about 219 kWh a year (0.6 times 365), roughly $39 at 18 cents per kWh.

A heat pump water heater needs only about a quarter of the daily electricity because it moves heat from the surrounding air instead of making it with a resistance element. That is why its yearly total, about 1,255 kWh, sits far below every tank in the table.

## Electric resistance vs gas and a heat pump

Comparing fuels fairly means pricing the same delivered heat. One million BTU of hot water takes about 293 kWh of electric resistance heat, or about 10 therms of gas before the heater's losses.

| Source | Cost per million BTU delivered |
|---|---|
| Electric resistance tank | $53.31 |
| Heat pump water heater | $15.23 |
| Natural gas (fuel only) | about $13.50 |

Resistance heat is 293 times 18.19 cents, about $53.31, since every unit of electricity makes one unit of heat. A heat pump divides that by its 3.5 efficiency (293 ÷ 3.5 is about 84 kWh), about $15.23. Gas fuel is about 10 therms times an assumed $1.35, about $13.50, though a storage tank's losses push its real cost higher.

A heat pump water heater lands close to gas, while a resistance tank costs roughly three to four times as much for the same heat. The full appliance comparison is in [gas vs. electric heating cost](/gas-vs-electric-heating-cost).

## Time-of-use rates

A tank water heater stores heat, so on a time-of-use plan it can do most of its heating off-peak and coast through the expensive hours. A timer or a utility program that controls the heater does this automatically. Tankless heaters can't, because they heat only while water flows.

Take the same 50-gallon tank for three people, about 389 kWh a month (12.8 kWh a day times 30.4), which costs about $70 on a flat 18 cents. Suppose a plan instead charges 30 cents peak and 12 cents off-peak, as an example; your plan's rates will differ.

Heating that 389 kWh entirely off-peak costs 389 times $0.12, about $47 a month, against 389 times $0.30, about $117, if it all ran during peak. Moving the tank's heating from peak to off-peak is worth about $70 a month here, which is why a timer or utility control pays off.

## How to cut the cost

- **Switch to a heat pump water heater**, which uses roughly a quarter of the electricity of a resistance tank.
- **Use efficient fixtures.** WaterSense showerheads use 2.0 gallons per minute or less, against 2.5 for standard ones.
- **Lower the thermostat to about 120°F** if yours is set higher.
- **Insulate the first few feet of hot water pipe** and fix dripping hot taps.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to run an electric water heater per month?

About $70 a month for a three-person household with a 50-gallon tank at 18 cents per kWh. It ranges from about $28 for one person to $133 for six.

### How many kWh does an electric water heater use per day?

About 12.8 kWh a day, or roughly 389 kWh a month, for three people with a 50-gallon tank, in the calculator's model. A heat pump water heater uses about 3.4 kWh for the same hot water.

### Is a heat pump water heater worth it?

For an all-electric home, usually. It cuts water heating cost by roughly three quarters, about $51 a month for a three-person household at 18 cents per kWh.

### Is electric or gas water heating cheaper?

At average prices, gas is cheaper than an electric resistance tank, and a heat pump water heater is about even with gas. See [gas vs. electric heating cost](/gas-vs-electric-heating-cost).

### How many kWh does a water heater use per hour?

A typical 4,500-watt resistance element draws 4.5 kWh for each hour it actively heats (4,500 watts times one hour, divided by 1,000). It does not run around the clock, though. Across a full day a 50-gallon tank for three people averages about 12.8 kWh, which is only a few hours of actual element time.

### How much does it cost to run a water heater per year?

About $850 a year for a three-person household with a 50-gallon resistance tank at the U.S. average 18.19 cents per kWh, which is its 4,675 kWh times that rate. The same usage runs from about $578 in North Dakota to $2,163 in Hawaii. A heat pump water heater cuts the national figure to roughly $228 a year.

### Does turning off the water heater save money?

Turning it off for a trip saves mainly the standby loss, about 1.4 kWh a day for a 50-gallon tank, roughly 25 cents at 18 cents per kWh. For a weekend away that is real but small, and the tank spends energy reheating when you return. The better everyday lever is lowering the thermostat toward 120°F, which trims both standby and heating losses without the inconvenience.

### What uses more electricity, a water heater or an air conditioner?

In an all-electric home a resistance water heater is one of the largest year-round loads: about 4,675 kWh a year for three people in this model, close to half of the 10,356 kWh an average U.S. home uses in total. Air conditioning varies widely with climate and house size, so which one is larger depends on where you live. A heat pump water heater shrinks the water-heating share to about 1,255 kWh a year.
