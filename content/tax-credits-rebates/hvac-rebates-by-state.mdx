# HVAC Rebates by State

The biggest HVAC rebates in 2026 come from two federal programs that each state runs itself: HEAR, for electric appliances such as heat pumps, and HOMES, for whole-home energy savings. The money is federal; the launch date, application process and timing are your state's.

**HEAR pays income-qualified households up to $8,000 for a heat pump and up to $14,000 in total, covering up to 100% of costs at or below 80% of area median income and up to 50% between 80% and 150%. The federal 25C and 25D tax credits ended for equipment installed after December 31, 2025. Whether your state's program is open, and how to apply, is on your state energy office's rebate page.**

## HEAR: electrification rebates

The Home Electrification and Appliance Rebates program pays toward these upgrades:

| Upgrade | Maximum rebate |
|---|---|
| Heat pump for heating and cooling | $8,000 |
| Electrical panel upgrade | $4,000 |
| Electrical wiring | $2,500 |
| Heat pump water heater | $1,750 |
| Insulation, air sealing and ventilation | $1,600 |
| Electric stove or cooktop | $840 |
| Heat pump clothes dryer | $840 |

The household total is capped at $14,000. Households at or below 80% of area median income can get up to 100% of the cost covered; households between 80% and 150% up to 50%. Replacing an existing heat pump isn't eligible, and gas furnaces and central air conditioners aren't covered.

## HOMES: whole-home savings rebates

The Home Efficiency Rebates program pays for projects that cut a home's energy use, by modeled or measured savings:

| Energy savings | Rebate | At or below 80% of area median income |
|---|---|---|
| 20% to 34% | $2,000 | $4,000 |
| 35% or more | $4,000 | $8,000 |

A household generally can't use HEAR and HOMES for the same upgrade. Together, the Inflation Reduction Act funded the two programs with $8.8 billion: $4.5 billion for HEAR and $4.3 billion for HOMES.

## Who qualifies: area median income

Eligibility depends on household income relative to the area median income (AMI) for your county and household size, which HUD publishes every year. Your state's program checks it, often through a simple income verification; HUD's income limits show the AMI for your area.

## What ended in 2025

The federal Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (25C), which paid 30% up to $2,000 for heat pumps, and the Residential Clean Energy Credit (25D) ended for property placed in service after December 31, 2025. See [HVAC tax credits 2026](/hvac-tax-credits-2026).

## Finding your state's program

State programs launch on their own schedules and can open, pause or run out of funds. To find what's available where you live:

1. **Your state energy office's rebate page** has the current status, eligible contractors and how to apply; DOE's Home Energy Rebate Programs page links to each state's program.
2. **DSIRE** lists state and utility incentives by state.
3. **Your electric and gas utilities** often run their own heat pump and efficiency rebates, which can stack with state programs.
4. **The Weatherization Assistance Program** provides free efficiency upgrades for income-eligible households through local agencies.

## How the rebates are paid

Most HEAR programs pay at the point of sale, through participating contractors or retailers who take the rebate off the price, rather than as a check afterward. Many require pre-approval and a participating contractor, so check before you sign a contract or start work.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is the heat pump rebate in 2026?

Up to $8,000 through HEAR for income-qualified households, depending on your state's program and your income relative to area median income.

### Is the federal heat pump tax credit still available?

No. The 25C credit ended for heat pumps installed after December 31, 2025. HEAR rebates, where your state's program is open, are the main federal support now.

### How do I know if my state's rebates are open?

Check your state energy office's rebate page, or DOE's Home Energy Rebate Programs page, which links to every state's program.

### Can I get a rebate for a gas furnace or central AC?

Not from HEAR, which covers electric appliances such as heat pumps. Some utilities offer rebates for high-efficiency furnaces and air conditioners; DSIRE lists them.
