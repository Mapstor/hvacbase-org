# BTU to CFM for Ductwork

A heating or cooling system's capacity is rated in BTU per hour, but ducts are sized by airflow, in cubic feet per minute (CFM). Converting one to the other is the step between choosing the equipment and sizing the ducts.

**For cooling, the usual design airflow is about 400 CFM per ton (12,000 BTU per hour), so a 3-ton system moves about 1,200 CFM. For heating, CFM = BTU per hour ÷ (1.08 × temperature rise): an 80,000 BTU per hour furnace output with a 50°F rise needs about 1,481 CFM.**

## The sensible heat formula

For air at standard conditions, **BTU per hour = 1.08 × CFM × temperature difference (°F)**. The 1.08 comes from the density and specific heat of air, times 60 minutes. Rearranged, **CFM = BTU per hour ÷ (1.08 × ΔT)**.

## Cooling: CFM per ton

Air conditioners are designed around a target airflow across the indoor coil, typically about 400 CFM per ton:

| System | Cooling capacity | Airflow at 400 CFM per ton |
|---|---|---|
| 2 tons | 24,000 BTU/hr | 800 CFM |
| 3 tons | 36,000 BTU/hr | 1,200 CFM |
| 4 tons | 48,000 BTU/hr | 1,600 CFM |

The equipment's specifications give its airflow range. Humid climates often run lower airflow per ton, so the coil runs colder and removes more moisture; dry climates often run higher, for more sensible cooling.

## Heating: temperature rise

A furnace's nameplate lists an allowed temperature-rise range. The airflow must keep the rise inside it: 80,000 BTU per hour of output needs about 1,481 CFM for a 50°F rise, or 1,058 CFM for a 70°F rise. Too little airflow overheats the heat exchanger; too much delivers lukewarm air.

## From CFM to duct size

Once you know the airflow, the duct size follows from the friction rate the blower can afford. At 0.08 inches of water per 100 feet, 1,200 CFM needs a round duct about 15.3 inches across, so a 16-inch duct; see the [ductwork sizing calculator](/ductwork-sizing-calculator). Each branch is sized for its room's share of the airflow, from a room-by-room load calculation (ACCA's Manual J and Manual D).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many CFM per ton?

About 400 CFM per ton is the usual design airflow for cooling; humid climates often use a little less, dry climates a little more.

### How do I convert BTU to CFM?

For heating or sensible cooling, CFM = BTU per hour ÷ (1.08 × temperature difference). For cooling equipment, multiply tons by about 400.

### How many CFM does a 3-ton AC need?

About 1,200 CFM at 400 CFM per ton.

### What duct size for 1,200 CFM?

About 16 inches round at a friction rate of 0.08 inches of water per 100 feet.
