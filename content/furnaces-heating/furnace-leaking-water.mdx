# Furnace Leaking Water?

Water around a furnace usually has a simple source: the drain that carries away condensation. High-efficiency furnaces make water as part of how they work, and when that water can't drain, it ends up on the floor.

**If you have a high-efficiency furnace (90% AFUE or higher, with white plastic vent pipes), the leak is most likely its condensate drain: a clogged trap, a blocked or disconnected drain line, or a failed condensate pump. Other common sources are the AC's indoor coil sitting on the furnace and a whole-house humidifier. On an older, standard-efficiency furnace, water usually points to a venting problem that needs a technician.**

## Turn it off first

Switch off the furnace at the thermostat and its power switch before working near it. Mop up the water, and if any gas smell or venting problem is possible, leave the area and call your gas utility or a technician. Keep carbon monoxide alarms working; see the [carbon monoxide detector guide](/carbon-monoxide-detector-guide).

## Which furnace do you have?

Identify the furnace type before troubleshooting, because the likely cause and the safe fix differ completely.

- **High-efficiency (condensing, 90% AFUE or higher):** two white plastic vent pipes run out through a side wall, one for exhaust and one for combustion air. These furnaces make condensate on purpose, so a drain is involved.
- **Standard-efficiency (around 80% AFUE):** a single metal flue runs up to the roof or into a chimney. These should not produce condensate at all, so standing water points to a venting fault.

If you see plastic vent pipes, work through the condensate drain below. If you see only a metal flue, skip to the standard-efficiency section and call a technician.

## Match the leak to when it happens

Noting when the water shows up narrows the cause quickly, because each source only produces water during specific operation.

| When the water appears | Most likely source | What to check first |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Only when the heat runs | High-efficiency condensate drain, or a furnace-mounted humidifier | The trap and drain line; the humidifier valve and drain pad |
| Only when the AC runs, in summer | The AC coil's condensate pan and drain above the furnace | The AC drain line and pan, not the furnace itself |
| Only during a hard freeze | A condensate drain line frozen where it crosses an unheated space | The line where it runs through a garage, attic, or crawl space |
| Around an 80% furnace with a metal flue, while heating | Exhaust condensing inside the flue, a venting fault | Nothing; shut it off and call a technician |

## High-efficiency furnace: the condensate drain

Condensing furnaces cool their exhaust until its water vapor condenses, recovering extra heat; that's how they reach 90% AFUE and higher. The water, which is mildly acidic, runs through a trap and a drain line to a floor drain or a condensate pump. Leaks come from:

- **A clogged trap or drain line**, from sediment or slime buildup.
- **A disconnected or cracked line**, often at a fitting.
- **A failed condensate pump**, or one whose float switch stuck.
- **A frozen drain line** where it runs through an unheated space.

Clearing a clogged line or reconnecting a hose is often a homeowner job; a cracked trap, a failed pump or water inside the furnace cabinet calls for a technician.

## Clearing a clogged condensate trap or drain line

On a high-efficiency furnace, a slow or blocked drain is the most common do-it-yourself fix. Slime and sediment build up in the trap and line over a season, backing water up until it overflows. Work with the furnace off at the thermostat and the power switch, as above.

- Put a bucket and towels under the trap, then disconnect the trap and the drain line at their fittings.
- Pull debris out of the trap, and run a wet/dry vacuum on the open end of the drain line to draw the clog toward you.
- Flush the trap and line with a mix of warm water and distilled white vinegar to break up the slime, then rinse with clean water.
- Reconnect every fitting snugly, pour water through the drain to confirm it runs freely, and restore power.

If the line clogs again within weeks, or you find a cracked trap, a failed pump, or water inside the cabinet, stop and call a technician.

## The AC coil

If your air conditioner's indoor coil sits on top of the furnace, its condensate pan and drain can leak in summer, and the water runs down onto the furnace. A clogged AC drain line or a cracked pan is the usual cause; the leak happens when the AC runs, not the heat.

## A whole-house humidifier

A humidifier mounted on the furnace's ductwork has its own water supply and drain. A stuck valve, clogged drain or worn pad can drip onto or around the furnace, usually during the heating season.

## Older standard-efficiency furnace

A standard furnace, around 80% AFUE with a metal flue, shouldn't produce condensate. Water around one usually means exhaust is cooling and condensing in the flue, often from a flue that's the wrong size or poorly connected. That's a venting problem: turn the furnace off and have a technician inspect it, because the same problem can let exhaust escape.

## When to call a professional

- Water inside the furnace cabinet or on its controls.
- Any sign of a venting or exhaust problem, or a carbon monoxide alarm.
- A cracked condensate trap, failed pump or repeated clogs.
- You can't find the source.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my furnace leaking water?

Most often, a clogged or disconnected condensate drain on a high-efficiency furnace. Other sources are the AC coil above it, a humidifier, or, on older furnaces, condensation in the flue.

### Is a furnace leaking water dangerous?

The water itself can damage the floor and the furnace's electronics. On an older furnace it can signal a venting problem, which can be a carbon monoxide risk; have it inspected.

### Can I fix a leaking furnace myself?

Clearing a clogged condensate line or reconnecting a hose often, yes, with the power off. Pumps, traps, venting and anything inside the cabinet are jobs for a technician.

### Why does my furnace leak water only when the AC runs?

The water is coming from the AC's indoor coil and condensate pan above the furnace, usually a clogged drain line.

### How do I unclog a furnace condensate drain?

With the furnace off, disconnect the trap and drain line, clear debris from the trap, and run a wet/dry vacuum on the line to pull out the clog. Flush the trap and line with warm water and distilled white vinegar to break up slime, reconnect every fitting, and pour water through to confirm it drains. Call a technician if it clogs again quickly, or if you find a cracked trap, a failed pump, or water inside the cabinet.

### How do I tell if I have a high-efficiency or standard furnace?

Look at the vent. Two white plastic pipes through a side wall mean a high-efficiency condensing furnace of 90% AFUE or higher, which makes condensate and has a drain. A single metal flue to a chimney or roof means a standard furnace of around 80% AFUE, which should not leak water at all.
