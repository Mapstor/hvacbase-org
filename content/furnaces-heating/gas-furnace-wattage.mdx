# How Many Watts Does a Gas Furnace Use?

A gas furnace burns gas for heat, but it still needs electricity to run: mostly for the blower that pushes air through the ducts, plus a small draft inducer, the igniter and the controls. That electricity matters for your bill, and even more if you want to run the furnace from a generator or battery in an outage.

**Most of a gas furnace's electricity goes to its blower. Find your furnace's draw from its rating plate (amps × 120 volts); as an example, a blower drawing 500 watts for 8 hours a day uses 120 kWh a month, about $22 at 18 cents per kWh. Newer furnaces use more efficient blower motors, under a DOE furnace fan standard.**

## Where the electricity goes

- **Blower motor:** moves air through the ducts, and runs the whole time the furnace heats; it's the largest load.
- **Draft inducer:** a small fan that vents combustion gases before and during each cycle.
- **Igniter:** a hot-surface igniter draws power for a short time at the start of each cycle.
- **Controls:** the control board and thermostat use very little.

## Finding your furnace's watts

The rating plate inside the furnace door lists the electrical rating, often as amps at 120 volts; watts ≈ amps × 120. The blower motor has its own label with its amps. The calculator above estimates typical draws by motor type; your furnace's labels give its actual figures.

To turn the plate's amps into watts, multiply by the voltage, because watts equal volts times amps on a standard circuit. A gas furnace runs on 120 volts, so the 500-watt blower example above works out to about 4.2 amps (500 ÷ 120 = 4.2). Reading the math the other way, a plate listing 5 amps at 120 volts is about 600 watts (5 × 120 = 600).

## What it costs to run

Cost = watts × hours ÷ 1,000 × your rate. A 500-watt blower running 8 hours a day for 30 days uses 120 kWh, about $21.60 at 18 cents per kWh. Many furnaces run the blower at lower speeds for much of the time, which uses less.

A variable-speed blower spends much of its time below full speed, so its real monthly cost usually sits below these full-speed figures.

## Blower motor types

Older furnaces typically use single-speed or multi-speed PSC (permanent split capacitor) motors. Newer furnaces use electronically commutated (ECM) or similar variable-speed motors, which draw much less at the low speeds they spend most of their time at. The DOE's energy conservation standard for furnace fans, in effect since 2019, pushed new furnaces toward these more efficient motors.

## Generators and batteries

A gas furnace can run from a generator or a battery during an outage, as long as it's connected safely through a transfer switch or interlock. The running load is small, mostly the blower at a few hundred watts plus the inducer and controls. The harder number to plan for is the start-up surge.

Anything with a motor pulls a brief inrush of current the instant it starts, several times its running draw, before settling. Size a generator for the running watts plus that largest surge, then add headroom; the [generator sizing guide](/what-size-generator-do-i-need) works a furnace blower through this step by step.

A battery or portable power station is sized by its stored energy in watt-hours. Divide the usable watt-hours by the blower's running watts for a rough runtime: an example 1,000-watt-hour station running the 500-watt blower lasts about 2 hours of continuous blower time (1,000 ÷ 500 = 2), and a 2,000-watt-hour station about 4 hours.

Real backup runs longer than that in practice, because the furnace cycles on and off rather than blowing continuously, and shorter because the inverter, inducer and igniter all draw their own power. The [home battery backup guide](/home-battery-backup-guide) covers continuous versus peak output, which matters as much as capacity for starting the blower.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many watts does a gas furnace use?

Mostly its blower; check the rating plate (amps × 120 volts). Older single-speed blowers draw more than newer variable-speed motors.

### Can a generator run a gas furnace?

Yes, through a transfer switch or interlock, if it covers the furnace's running watts and the blower's start-up surge.

### How much does it cost to run a furnace blower?

About $21.60 a month for a 500-watt blower running 8 hours a day at 18 cents per kWh.

### Does a gas furnace work without electricity?

No. Without power, the blower, inducer, igniter and controls can't run, so the furnace won't heat.

### Can a small inverter generator run a gas furnace?

Usually yes, because a furnace's running load is only a few hundred watts, but the generator still has to cover the blower's brief starting surge as well as its running watts. Check the furnace rating plate against the generator's running and surge (starting) watt ratings. The [generator sizing guide](/what-size-generator-do-i-need) shows how to add the surge.

### How long will a portable power station run a gas furnace?

Divide the station's watt-hours by the blower's running watts: an example 1,000-watt-hour station runs a 500-watt blower about 2 hours of continuous blowing (1,000 ÷ 500 = 2). Because the furnace cycles on and off, real backup usually stretches longer, while inverter losses and the inducer and igniter shorten it.
