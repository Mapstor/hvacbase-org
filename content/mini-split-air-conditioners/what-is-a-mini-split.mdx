# What Is a Mini Split AC? How Ductless Systems Work

You've heard the term "mini split," maybe a neighbor got one, or a contractor suggested it instead of central air, and you're trying to figure out: **what actually is it, how is it different from regular AC, and would it make sense for my house?** Especially if you've got a room that's always too hot or too cold, or a home with no ductwork, the idea sounds appealing but unfamiliar.

Here's the plain-English rundown. A mini split is a type of heating-and-cooling system that skips the ductwork, and that one difference drives most of its advantages (and a couple of its trade-offs). We'll cover exactly what it is, how it works, why it's so efficient, and when it's the right choice versus regular central air. The efficiency figures here come from ENERGY STAR and the Department of Energy, linked at the bottom.

**A mini split (or "ductless mini split") is a heat pump system that heats and cools without ductwork.** Instead of pushing air through ducts, it mounts one or more indoor units directly on your walls or ceilings, each connected by a thin refrigerant line to an outdoor unit. Because there are no ducts to leak, and because it uses variable-speed (inverter) technology, it's typically more efficient than central systems, and it lets you set different temperatures in different rooms.

## How a mini split actually works

The core idea: a mini split is a **heat pump**, which means it *moves* heat rather than burning fuel to create it. In summer it moves heat out of your home; in winter it moves heat from the outdoor air into your home (yes, even cold air contains heat a heat pump can extract). That's why one system does both heating and cooling.

The "ductless" part is what makes it different from central air. Per ENERGY STAR, here's the setup:

- **One or more indoor units** (called "heads") mount on an interior wall or ceiling in the rooms you want to condition.
- **An outdoor unit** sits outside, like a central AC's condenser.
- **A thin refrigerant line** connects them, running through just a small (~3-inch) hole in an exterior wall. No bulky ductwork required.

The outdoor unit extracts heat from the air and the refrigerant carries it directly to the indoor head, which delivers conditioned air right into the room. In summer, the whole process runs in reverse to cool. Because the conditioned air comes straight out of the head in the room, none of it is lost traveling through ducts, which is the key to the efficiency advantage below. For a closer look at the refrigeration cycle behind this, see our deeper guide on [how a mini split works](/how-does-a-mini-split-work).

## Why mini splits are so efficient

Two things make mini splits stand out on efficiency, and both are documented:

**1. No duct losses.** This is the big one. According to **ENERGY STAR**, ductwork can waste **more than 30% of a home's energy use for space conditioning**, air leaking out of duct seams, or losing heat as it travels through unconditioned attics and crawlspaces. A mini split eliminates that entirely by skipping ducts. All the conditioned air ends up where you want it.

**2. Inverter (variable-speed) technology.** Instead of the older "full blast or off" cycling of many central systems, a mini split's compressor adjusts its speed to precisely match demand, ramping down to sip energy once the room is comfortable. This variable-speed operation produces higher efficiency ratings.

The numbers back it up. Per the **DOE**, ductless mini splits achieve **SEER2 cooling ratings from about 15.2 up to 35**, while ducted heat pumps typically top out around 25. On the heating side, an ENERGY STAR-certified mini split can deliver up to about **three times more heat energy than the electricity it uses**, and can use up to **60% less energy than standard electric resistance heating** (like baseboard heaters), per ENERGY STAR.

## The other big advantage: zoning

Because each indoor head operates independently, you get **room-by-room temperature control.** You can cool the bedroom without freezing the living room, or heat only the rooms you're using. With a central system, you're conditioning the whole house to one thermostat's reading, which is why you get hot and cold spots. Mini splits let you stop paying to condition empty rooms, and fix the "one room is always uncomfortable" problem directly.

You can add heads for more rooms (a "multi-zone" system), and they come in wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and even concealed ducted styles.

## When a mini split makes sense (and when it doesn't)

**A mini split is often a great fit if:**
- **Your home has no ductwork** (older homes with radiators or baseboard heat), retrofitting ducts is expensive and disruptive, and a mini split avoids it.
- **You have costly electric heat** (baseboard, electric furnace, wall heaters), the efficiency gain over resistance heating is large.
- **You have a problem room**, an addition, converted garage, sunroom, or a space that's always too hot or cold that your central system can't handle well.
- **You want zoned control** and are tired of hot and cold spots.

**Central air might be the better choice if:**
- **Your home already has good ductwork** in decent condition, then a high-efficiency ducted system may make more sense.
- **You're conditioning a very large open space** where multiple heads would be impractical.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a mini split system?**
A ductless heat pump that heats and cools without ductwork. It uses one or more indoor units (heads) mounted on walls or ceilings, connected by a thin refrigerant line to an outdoor unit. Because it skips ducts and uses variable-speed technology, it's typically more efficient than central systems and allows room-by-room temperature control.

**How is a mini split different from regular AC?**
The main difference is no ductwork, and a mini split also heats (it's a heat pump, not just an AC). Central AC pushes air through ducts (which lose energy); a mini split delivers conditioned air directly from a head in each room. Mini splits also offer per-room zoning, while central systems run to one thermostat.

**Are mini splits energy efficient?**
Yes, notably so. They eliminate duct losses (which ENERGY STAR says can waste more than 30% of conditioning energy) and use variable-speed compressors. Per the DOE, they reach SEER2 ratings up to about 35, and ENERGY STAR says they can use up to 60% less energy than electric resistance heating.

**Can a mini split heat and cool a whole house?**
Yes. With enough indoor heads (a multi-zone system), a mini split can condition an entire home. They're also commonly used to supplement an existing system or handle specific rooms. The right number and size of heads depends on your home's layout and each room's load.

**Do mini splits work in cold weather?**
Modern cold-climate mini splits perform well even in freezing temperatures, extracting heat from cold outdoor air. The old reputation of heat pumps struggling in the cold is increasingly outdated for cold-climate-rated models.
