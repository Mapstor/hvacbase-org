# Mini Split Brands Ranked: 3 AHRI-Verified Picks (2026)

Three brands carry units with **specs traceable to the official AHRI Certificate of Product Ratings or the manufacturer spec sheet** in 2026: Daikin (Aurora 12K), Senville (LETO SENL-12CD), and Mitsubishi (MSZ-FS12NA). Other brands appear on prior comparison tables across this site with values that have not been verified against primary sources — those rankings have been removed from this page until we can pull each model's AHRI cert.

The ordering below is by **certification status and current availability**, not just raw SEER2. The unit with the highest certified SEER2 (Mitsubishi MSZ-FS12NA at 26.3) is ranked third because Mitsubishi has stopped producing it (AHRI cert status: Production Stopped) and it runs R-410A — the refrigerant being phased out under the EPA's AIM Act rule. The Daikin Aurora 12K leads because it is AHRI-certified, **Active**, and ships with the current low-GWP refrigerant (R-32).

Rankings are based on AHRI-certified efficiency ratings and manufacturer specifications. No brand paid to be included.

## Why this ordering (transparency)

The Mitsubishi MSZ-FS12NA has the highest AHRI-certified SEER2 on this page (26.3). It's ranked third anyway because:

1. **Mitsubishi has stopped producing it.** The AHRI certificate lists status: Production Stopped. The unit is still sold from existing distributor inventory but no longer manufactured.
2. **It runs R-410A.** As of January 1, 2025, R-410A may no longer be produced or imported for new residential AC/HP equipment under the EPA's AIM Act rule. R-410A pre-2025 inventory may still be installed in 2026 in most states (per the EPA's amended final rule effective July 27, 2026); **New York codified the original Jan 1, 2026 install ban**.

The Daikin Aurora 12K leads because it is AHRI-certified, **Active**, and on the current low-GWP refrigerant (R-32, GWP 675 — under the EPA's 700 threshold).

| **Status** | **Active** | **AHRI Certificate** |

**Heat-transfer angle (why EVI / flash-injection maintains capacity in cold):** at outdoor temperatures below ~25°F, refrigerant arriving at the compressor inlet is at very low density, and a stock compressor can't move enough mass to deliver useful heat. **Enhanced vapor injection (EVI) / flash injection** pulls a side stream of partially-condensed refrigerant back into a mid-stage port on the compressor — increasing the mass flow rate without changing the compressor's physical displacement. That's what lets the Aurora deliver 100% rated heating capacity at 5°F and still operate at -13°F.

**Heat-transfer angle (why a budget unit can still hit 21+ SEER2):** modern variable-speed inverter compressors are no longer the cost frontier they were a decade ago. The compressor castings, EEV control boards, and BLDC fan motors that make 21 SEER2 possible are now widely available across the supply chain. The trade-offs at this price tier are **build refinement** (heavier-gauge copper, tighter refrigerant-charge tolerances, polished controls software) and **long-term reliability** — not the headline SEER2 number.

> **Earlier copy on this site claimed the MSZ-FS achieved 40.1 SEER2 — that value was a fabrication.** The AHRI-certified SEER2 is 26.30. No residential mini split reaches 40 SEER2; the market ceiling is roughly 33–35 SEER2.

**Heat-transfer angle (why 26.3 SEER2):** The MSZ-FS pairs a large indoor-coil heat-exchanger area with very fine low-speed inverter modulation. SEER2 rewards extended part-load operation, and a unit that can sit at 20–30% capacity without cycling spends nearly all its energy moving heat rather than restarting the compressor.

**Production Stopped + R-410A caveat:** Mitsubishi's current new lineup has moved off this unit. The AIM Act's manufacturing cutoff (Jan 1, 2025) means new R-410A residential AC/HP equipment can no longer be produced or imported in the U.S. R-410A may still be installed from pre-2025 inventory in most states (per the EPA amended final rule, effective July 27, 2026); **New York codified the original Jan 1, 2026 install ban**. Service R-410A remains available for existing systems but is tightening.

## Refrigerant Transition (2025–2026 AIM Act)

Critical context for any 2026 purchase decision:

1. **Manufacturing cutoff: Jan 1, 2025.** Manufacturers may no longer produce or import new residential split-system AC or heat pumps using R-410A. Equipment now ships with **R-454B (GWP 466)** or **R-32 (GWP 675)** — both A2L, both under the EPA's 700-GWP threshold.
2. **Installation: R-410A is not banned.** Equipment manufactured before Jan 1, 2025 may still be installed (EPA amended final rule, effective July 27, 2026) until existing inventory depletes. Existing systems can be serviced and recharged indefinitely.
3. **State caveat:** Some states are stricter. **New York codified the original Jan 1, 2026 install ban.** Check your state and local code.

For the three shortlisted units: Daikin Aurora is **R-32**; Senville LETO is **R-454B**; Mitsubishi MSZ-FS12NA is **R-410A** (the phased-out refrigerant — see #3 caveat).
