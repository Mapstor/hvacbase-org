# MrCool 3rd Gen vs 4th Gen: Which DIY Mini Split to Buy?

The MrCool DIY 4th Gen outperforms the 3rd Gen in every measurable category: 10% higher efficiency (22 vs 20 SEER2 for the 12K model), 9°F colder heating capability (-4°F vs 5°F), 2 dB quieter indoor operation, and improved build quality. The 4th Gen costs $200–$300 more per unit. For most buyers, the 4th Gen is worth the premium — but the 3rd Gen remains a solid value if you find it discounted.

<Callout type="warning">
**5th Gen is the current generation.** MrCool now sells a 5th Gen DIY line that is the current production model — **23.5 SEER2** with **R-454B refrigerant** (low-GWP, A2L, compliant with the EPA AIM Act manufacturing cutoff), cold-climate certified. Per locked-spec data, the 4th Gen runs **R-410A** (legacy refrigerant, manufacturer cutoff Jan 1, 2025) — pre-2025 inventory may still be installed in most states (EPA amended final rule, effective Jul 27, 2026), but the 4th Gen is no longer the current line. This comparison covers 3rd vs 4th Gen because both remain in distribution; if you're buying new in 2026, evaluate the 5th Gen too. Source: MrCool manufacturer pages (mrcool.com).
</Callout>

## 5th Gen: What Changed From 4th Gen

The jump from 4th Gen (R-410A) to 5th Gen (R-454B) is driven by the EPA's Technology Transitions Rule under the American Innovation and Manufacturing (AIM) Act, which caps the Global Warming Potential (GWP) of refrigerants in new residential air-conditioning and heat-pump manufacturing at 700 (40 CFR Part 84, Subpart B). R-410A carries a commonly cited GWP of roughly 2,088; R-454B lands near 466 on the same reference basis. That gap is the entire reason the industry pivoted — no residential mini-split manufactured for the U.S. market after January 1, 2025 may use R-410A under the rule's compliance date for that end-use category.

**Design carryovers.** The 5th Gen keeps the pre-charged line set and quick-connect fitting design that defines the DIY line. The compressor and coil are sized for the different pressure and mass-flow characteristics of R-454B, but the outward form factor and installation workflow are largely unchanged from the 4th Gen — the DIY value proposition is intact.

**Efficiency and cold-climate ratings.** MrCool publishes a 23.5 SEER2 headline figure for the 5th Gen 12K model on mrcool.com (the Callout above quotes this). Model-specific HSPF2, EER2, and low-ambient heating-capacity numbers vary by size and should be pulled from the AHRI-certified certificate for the exact indoor+outdoor pairing rather than from marketing copy — the certificate is the only value that governs tax, rebate, and warranty claims.

## Refrigerant Service Implications

R-454B is classified **A2L** under ASHRAE Standard 34 — lower flammability, lower toxicity. That classification changes three practical things for anyone opening a 5th Gen circuit compared with a 3rd or 4th Gen R-410A system:

- **Technician training.** EPA Section 608 certification is still the baseline for handling either refrigerant, but A2L-specific training on ignition-source control, ventilation, and per-room charge limits is now standard. UL 60335-2-40 — the equipment safety standard adopted for A2L residential units — sets a maximum refrigerant charge per conditioned room area that must be confirmed on site during installation.
- **Flare-connection torque.** MrCool DIY quick-connects ship pre-flared, but any field-cut lineset extension has to be torqued to the manufacturer's published value on a calibrated wrench. Flare joints are the dominant leak path on mini-splits, and A2L systems are less forgiving of slow leaks because the total legal charge is size-limited by room area. Torque specs live in the installation manual for the specific model.
- **Leak detection.** Halogenated leak detectors used for R-410A also read R-454B, but shops doing A2L work commonly add a combustible-gas monitor to the tool kit. Jurisdictions adopting the 2024 International Mechanical Code (IMC) reference A2L handling requirements directly.

**Legacy R-410A service.** The AIM Act phases down virgin R-410A production and import, not service of existing systems — the same regulatory pattern that governed R-22 after its 2010 manufacturing cutoff. Reclaimed R-410A remains legal to charge into an existing 3rd or 4th Gen unit indefinitely. Per-pound top-off pricing is widely expected to climb through the late 2020s on the R-22 trajectory; a leak-free system never needs a top-off.

## Should You Wait for the 5th Gen?

The decision comes down to three variables: how long you plan to keep the system, how much you value refrigerant future-proofing, and the real delivered-price gap at your distributor.

**Hold horizon under 8 years.** A discounted 4th Gen (R-410A) can be the cheaper lifetime buy if the price gap versus 5th Gen is roughly $300 or more per head and you plan to replace or move before reclaimed-R-410A pricing peaks. The 3rd Gen calculus is similar but carries additional risk on parts availability rather than refrigerant supply — the older the generation, the shorter the runway on control boards and proprietary components.

**Hold horizon 10+ years.** The 5th Gen (R-454B) becomes the more defensible choice. Legacy-refrigerant service costs typically rise through the phase-down window, the modest published SEER2 uplift compounds across the ownership period, and low-GWP equipment aligns better with codes and utility rebate programs that increasingly reference AIM Act–compliant refrigerants.

**Cold-climate priority.** If your winter design temperature is below 5 °F, the 3rd-to-4th generational jump (5 °F down to −4 °F per the spec table above) matters far more than the 4th-to-5th jump. Confirm the specific 5th Gen model's low-ambient heating capacity on its AHRI certificate before assuming further improvement rather than parity.

**Warranty and channel support.** The 5th Gen is MrCool's current production line, so authorized-dealer inventory, parts, and factory technical support have the longest tail on that generation. A heavily discounted 3rd Gen from a non-authorized channel is the highest-risk buy on both refrigerant and warranty axes — a point worth weighting against any headline price advantage.
