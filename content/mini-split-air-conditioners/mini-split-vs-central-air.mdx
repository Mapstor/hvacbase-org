# Mini Split vs Central Air: Which Is Better? (8 Key Differences)

You're deciding between a mini split and central air, and you want a straight answer about which is actually better for your situation, not a generic "it depends" that helps no one. The honest truth is that it *does* depend, but on a small number of specific things you can check, and this guide walks through exactly what they are.

We'll compare the two head-to-head on the eight things that actually matter (efficiency, cost, installation, zoning, aesthetics, noise, maintenance, and resale), and give you a clear decision framework. Every efficiency figure is sourced to the DOE and ENERGY STAR, linked at the bottom, and we name no specific brands.

**The short answer: if your home already has ductwork in good condition, central air is usually the simpler and often cheaper choice. If you don't have ducts, need to condition specific rooms, or want room-by-room temperature control, a mini split delivers better efficiency and value.** The single biggest factor is whether you already have usable ductwork, that one thing tips most decisions.

## The core difference: ducts

Everything flows from one distinction. Central air pushes conditioned air through ductwork to every room; a mini split delivers it straight from an indoor unit mounted in the room, with no ducts.

That matters more than it sounds, because ducts leak. **A typical duct system loses a substantial share of the energy moving through it**, ENERGY STAR puts duct losses at as much as 20% of the air a system moves, and the DOE cites typical energy losses of 20 to 30%, from leaks, poor insulation, and connections running through unconditioned attics and crawlspaces. So a central AC rated at, say, SEER2 16 delivers noticeably less than that in practice once duct losses are counted. A mini split has no ducts, so its rated efficiency is much closer to what you actually get, and combined with inverter compressor technology, that's why mini splits are generally the more efficient option.

## The 8 key differences, head to head

| 1 | Energy efficiency | Higher SEER2, no duct losses | Lower effective efficiency after 20–30% duct loss | Mini split |

The pattern: **mini splits win on efficiency, ductless installation, and zoning; central air wins on aesthetics, cost-when-you-already-have-ducts, and buyer familiarity.** Which set matters more is what decides it for your home.

## Efficiency, in more detail

Mini splits are more efficient for two compounding reasons: no duct losses (the 20 to 30% the DOE cites), and inverter compressors that modulate output to match demand instead of cycling fully on and off. In real-world conditions this adds up to meaningfully lower energy use than a comparable ducted system.

But two honest caveats keep this from being a blanket "mini splits always cost less to run":
- **If you're comparing against central air with good existing ducts**, and especially against cheap natural gas heating, the operating-cost gap narrows.
- **On the heating side specifically**, a mini split heat pump's advantage depends on your local electricity-to-gas price ratio, the same crossover math we cover in our [heat pump guide](/heat-pump-guide). On cheap gas, a high-efficiency furnace can be competitive to run.

## Cost: it hinges entirely on your ductwork

This is where the decision usually gets made, and it splits cleanly:

**If you DON'T have ductwork:** central air requires installing ducts, which is a major expense on top of the equipment, often making a mini split the clearly cheaper path to cooling those spaces. Retrofitting ducts into a finished home is disruptive and costly.

**If you DO have good ductwork:** you can simply replace the central unit, which is typically cheaper than installing a multi-zone mini split system, so central air often wins on upfront cost.

For per-scenario pricing, see our [mini split installation cost guide](/mini-split-installation-cost). And note on incentives: the **federal 25C/25D tax credits expired for installs placed in service after December 31, 2025** (under the OBBBA), but state and utility rebates and the IRA HEAR/HOMES programs remain active in 2026 for qualifying heat pump systems, worth checking before you decide.

## Zoning: the mini split's signature advantage

With central air, one thermostat typically governs the whole house, which is why you get hot and cold spots and pay to condition empty rooms. A mini split gives each indoor head its own control, so you can cool the bedroom without freezing the living room, or heat only the rooms you're using. If uneven temperatures or "one room is always uncomfortable" is your problem, zoning is a direct fix, and it's central air's biggest weakness.

## When each one wins (the decision framework)

**Choose a mini split if:**
- Your home has no ductwork, or the existing ducts are in poor condition.
- You're adding AC to specific rooms, an addition, a garage, or a finished basement.
- You want room-by-room temperature control.
- Efficiency and lower operating cost are priorities and you're replacing electric resistance, oil, or propane heat.

**Choose central air if:**
- You already have ductwork in good condition and just need to replace the system.
- You're conditioning many rooms with similar usage and want one simple whole-house system.
- You prefer no visible indoor equipment.
- Lowest upfront cost matters most and the ducts are already there.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a mini split cheaper than central air?**
It depends entirely on your ductwork. Without existing ducts, a mini split is usually cheaper than central air (which would require installing ductwork). With good existing ducts, replacing a central unit is often cheaper than a multi-zone mini split. Operating cost also favors mini splits in most cases, but the gap narrows against cheap natural gas.

**Are mini splits more efficient than central air?**
Generally yes. Mini splits avoid the 20 to 30% duct losses the DOE attributes to typical duct systems, and their inverter compressors modulate to match demand. That combination means lower real-world energy use than a comparable ducted system.

**Do mini splits heat as well as cool?**
Yes, most are heat pumps that do both. Cold-climate models heat efficiently well below freezing. Whether heating costs less than a gas furnace depends on your local electricity-to-gas price ratio (see our heat pump guide for that math).

**Is central air better for resale value?**
Central air is universally expected and familiar to buyers, which gives it a slight edge in general resale terms. Mini splits are increasingly accepted, and in homes that never had AC at all, adding a mini split is a clear value and comfort upgrade.

**Which is better for a home without ductwork?**
A mini split, in most cases. Adding ductwork for central air to a home that lacks it is expensive and disruptive, while a mini split delivers conditioned air without ducts and adds zoning as a bonus.
