# Is Air Duct Cleaning Worth It?

Duct cleaning is sold as a health and efficiency upgrade, but the evidence for routine cleaning is thin. The EPA's guidance is specific about when it makes sense, and most homes don't meet those conditions.

**The EPA says duct cleaning has never been shown to prevent health problems, and isn't needed routinely. It's worth considering when there's substantial visible mold growth inside the ducts or other parts of the system, ducts infested with insects or rodents, or so much dust and debris that it blows out of the supply registers.**

## What the EPA says

Dust in ducts mostly sticks to the duct walls rather than entering the living space, and the EPA notes that studies don't show dirty ducts raise dust levels in homes. Cleaning that disturbs the dust without containing it can actually release more into the house for a while.

## When cleaning is worth it

- **Visible mold growth** inside hard-surfaced ducts or on other components such as the coil, and the moisture problem behind it fixed first. See [how to identify mold](/how-to-identify-mold).
- **Vermin:** ducts infested with insects or rodents.
- **Heavy debris:** dust and debris clogging the ducts or blowing out of registers, such as after a renovation.

## When it isn't

- **Routine cleaning** on a schedule, with no specific problem.
- **To save energy:** clean ducts don't fix leaky, poorly insulated ones; sealing does. See [duct leakage testing](/duct-leakage-testing).
- **For allergies alone:** a better filter and humidity control usually help more.

## Worth it or not, at a glance

The EPA ties the decision to a specific problem, not a calendar. The table sorts the common reasons people consider cleaning into the ones the EPA supports and the ones it does not.

| Situation | EPA's position | Clean the ducts? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visible mold inside hard-surface ducts or on the coil | Clean, but fix the moisture source first | Yes |
| Insects or rodents nesting in the ducts | A valid reason to clean | Yes |
| Dust or debris blowing out of the registers, such as after a renovation | A valid reason to clean | Yes |
| Routine cleaning on a schedule, no symptom | Not shown to be necessary | No |
| Hoping to lower energy bills | Cleaning dust off duct walls is not the fix | No |
| Allergies or general dust, nothing visible in the ducts | A better filter and humidity control usually help more | No |

## What cleaning changes, and what the ads promise

Removing loose dust from duct walls does not touch the two things that actually drive duct energy loss: leaks and missing insulation. Those are sealing and insulation jobs, not cleaning jobs.

Sealing leaky ducts can recover as much as 20% of heating and cooling efficiency, according to ENERGY STAR, and the DOE puts duct air losses at about 30% of a cooling system's energy. A cleaning changes neither figure.

The clearest energy win in this part of the house is the filter, not the ducts. The DOE says a clean filter can lower an air conditioner's energy use by 5 to 15%, so a cheap monthly filter check beats a periodic duct cleaning on efficiency. See the [filter-change guide](/how-often-change-hvac-filter).

On mold, cleaning only helps if mold is actually growing, and only after the moisture source is corrected. Keep indoor humidity between 30% and 50%, since mold can take hold above 60% within 24 to 48 hours. Spraying a biocide over a damp duct treats the symptom, not the cause.

## What a proper cleaning involves

The EPA advises that a cleaning should cover the whole system, not just the ducts: supply and return ducts and registers, grilles and diffusers, the heat exchanger, cooling coil, drain pan, fan and housing, and the air handler. The contractor should put the system under negative pressure with a vacuum so dislodged dust is collected, and protect the house from it. Ask for references, and be wary of offers that clean an entire house for a suspiciously low price.

## Biocides and sealants

Some services offer to spray chemical biocides or sealants inside ducts. The EPA notes that little is known about their effectiveness and possible risks in HVAC systems, and recommends understanding what will be applied, and why, before agreeing. Never let anyone apply a product that isn't registered for use in ducts.

## Vetting a contractor and the usual red flags

Turn the EPA's advice into a short checklist before you hire. Confirm the quote covers the whole system described above, not just the duct runs, and ask how the crew will contain the dust they dislodge.

Be wary of a price that cleans an entire house for very little. A quote far below the going rate usually points to a thin, duct-only scope rather than the whole-system job described above.

Decline any biocide or sealant that is not registered for use in HVAC ducts, and get the product name and reason in writing first. Skip anyone who claims a routine cleaning will cut your bills or prevent illness.

Cost tracks the work rather than a flat rate. A larger system with more supply and return registers, and harder access to the air handler, takes longer than a small one, and a whole-system cleaning is more involved than a duct-only pass.

## Better first steps

- **Replace the filter** on schedule, with the highest MERV your system handles; see the [MERV rating chart](/merv-rating-chart).
- **Fix moisture** at the coil and drain pan, and keep indoor humidity between 30% and 50%.
- **Seal and insulate leaky ducts** in attics and crawl spaces.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is air duct cleaning worth it?

Only for visible mold, vermin or heavy debris, according to the EPA. Routine cleaning hasn't been shown to improve health or air quality.

### How often should air ducts be cleaned?

There's no recommended schedule; clean them when one of those specific problems appears.

### Does duct cleaning improve energy efficiency?

Not in any proven way. Sealing leaks and insulating ducts in unconditioned spaces saves energy; cleaning dust off the walls doesn't.

### Can duct cleaning make air quality worse?

It can for a while, if dust is disturbed without being captured. A proper job keeps the system under vacuum and contains the debris.

### How much does air duct cleaning cost?

Cost depends on the size of the system, the number of supply and return registers, and how easy the air handler is to reach, so there is no single flat rate. A whole-system cleaning that includes the coil, drain pan and blower runs higher than a duct-only pass. Treat a quote far below the going rate as a sign the scope is thin rather than a bargain.

### Is duct cleaning a scam?

Not inherently, but the EPA has never shown routine cleaning to prevent health problems or improve air quality, so paying for it on a schedule rarely delivers what the ads promise. A very low advertised price often signals a thin, duct-only scope rather than the whole-system cleaning the EPA describes. It is worth paying for when there is real mold, vermin or heavy debris, and little else.
