# HVAC Maintenance Cost

Regular maintenance is the cheapest thing you can do for a heating and cooling system. ENERGY STAR recommends a pre-season checkup by a contractor, the cooling system in spring and the heating system in fall, plus filter changes you handle yourself.

**A standard tune-up is an hour or two of a technician's time, so the price tracks local labor rates and cost of living more than the equipment itself. A heat pump or mini split costs more over a year than an air conditioner or furnace, because it is serviced in both the heating and cooling seasons rather than once.** Local rates vary widely, so treat any quote as a starting point and ask what the visit includes.

## Tune-up costs by system

| System | Visits per year | Relative cost |
|---|---|---|
| Central air conditioner | One, before cooling season | Lowest; a single short visit |
| Gas furnace | One, before heating season | Low; close to an air conditioner |
| Air conditioner and gas furnace | Two, one per season | Two single visits combined |
| Air-source heat pump | Two, heating and cooling | Higher; the same unit is checked twice a year |
| Ductless mini split | Two, and each indoor head adds work | Higher; scales with the number of heads |
| Boiler | One, before heating season | Low to moderate |
| Oil furnace | One, before heating season | Moderate; more involved burner service |
| Geothermal heat pump | Two, heating and cooling | Highest; specialized ground-loop system |

After-hours and emergency calls typically cost more. Repairs found during a tune-up, such as a capacitor or refrigerant leak, are extra.

## What drives the price in your area

What you pay is mostly labor. A tune-up is an hour or two of a technician's time, so the price tracks local labor rates and cost of living more than the equipment itself.

Higher-cost metros and much of the Northeast run higher, while the South and rural areas run lower. A long drive to your home can add a trip charge, and nights, weekends and peak-season calls usually carry a premium.

Two visits cost more than one for the same reason, not because the second trip uses more parts. You are paying again for the technician's time and travel.

## Do the savings cover the tune-up?

Maintenance pays for itself mainly by keeping the system efficient and heading off breakdowns. The clearest figure is the filter: the DOE says a clean filter can lower an air conditioner's energy use by 5 to 15%, and coil cleaning and a correct refrigerant charge protect that same efficiency.

Put that against a running cost. A 3-ton air conditioner at the federal-minimum 14.3 SEER2, run 1,500 full-load hours a year at 18 cents per kWh, uses 3 × 12,000 × 1,500 ÷ (14.3 × 1,000), which is about 3,776 kWh, or roughly $680 a year to cool. The hours are an example; a shorter northern season uses fewer and a long southern one more.

A 5 to 15% efficiency loss on that $680 works out to about $34 to $102 a year. At the top of that band it can approach what a single air-conditioner tune-up costs, before counting the repairs a visit catches early, such as a clogged condensate drain or a weak capacitor.

## What a tune-up should include

ENERGY STAR's checklist for a contractor visit includes:

- **Thermostat:** check settings and operation.
- **Electrical:** tighten connections and measure voltage and current on motors.
- **Moving parts:** lubricate where needed; check belts.
- **Condensate drain:** clear it, so it doesn't overflow or grow mold.
- **Controls:** check that the system starts, runs and shuts off properly.
- **Cooling:** clean the evaporator and condenser coils and check the refrigerant level.
- **Heating:** check gas or oil connections, gas pressure, burner combustion and the heat exchanger.

A visit that skips most of this isn't a full tune-up. Ask what's included before booking.

## Service plans vs. paying per visit

Many contractors sell annual plans that bundle two tune-ups with perks such as priority scheduling or waived diagnostic fees. Compare the plan's yearly price with what two individual tune-ups would cost you locally, and read what it actually covers; a plan pays off mainly if you'd use its repair discounts or priority service.

The break-even is easy to pin down. Add up what two separate tune-ups, one for the air conditioner and one for the gas furnace, would cost you locally. A plan priced below that is cheaper than paying per visit even before any perks, while one priced above it only pays off if its repair discounts, waived fees or priority service close the gap.

## A yearly maintenance budget

For a typical home with central air conditioning and a gas furnace, the recurring cost is two pre-season tune-ups a year plus the filter changes you handle yourself. Those two tune-ups are the bulk of the recurring cost.

Filters are the smallest line in that budget, so the two tune-ups set the yearly figure. A heat pump home runs higher, because the same unit is serviced in both the heating and cooling seasons rather than once.

## Maintenance you can do yourself

- **Change or clean the filter** as the system's maker recommends. The DOE says clean filters can lower an air conditioner's energy use by 5 to 15%.
- **Keep the outdoor unit clear** of leaves, grass and debris, with a couple of feet of open space around it.
- **Keep supply and return vents unblocked.**
- **Watch the condensate line** for clogs during cooling season.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does an HVAC tune-up cost?

A tune-up is an hour or two of a technician's time, so what you pay tracks local labor rates more than the equipment. An air conditioner or furnace visit sits at the low end, while a heat pump or mini split costs more because it is serviced in both seasons; ask what the visit includes before booking.

### How often should HVAC be serviced?

ENERGY STAR recommends a pre-season checkup: the cooling system in spring and the heating system in fall. Heat pumps, which do both, get both.

### Is an HVAC maintenance plan worth it?

If it costs less than two individual tune-ups and you'd use its perks, often yes. Compare the yearly price with paying per visit.

### What does an AC tune-up include?

Coil cleaning, refrigerant and electrical checks, clearing the condensate drain, lubricating moving parts and testing the controls and thermostat.

### Is a rock-bottom HVAC tune-up legit?

A very low price can still buy a real tune-up, since a basic visit is mostly labor. The risk is a stripped visit that skips coil cleaning or the refrigerant and electrical checks, so ask what is included and be cautious with a very cheap call that then produces a long repair list.

### How much does it cost to recharge refrigerant?

A recharge price depends on the refrigerant type and how much the system lost, and a correctly sealed system should not need topping up. Needing refrigerant means there is a leak, so the real job is finding and fixing that leak; adding refrigerant without it just delays the same failure.

### Why are heat pump tune-ups more expensive?

A heat pump both heats and cools through one refrigerant circuit, so it is checked in both the cooling and heating seasons rather than once a year. That means two visits, which is why its yearly total runs above a single air-conditioner or furnace tune-up.
