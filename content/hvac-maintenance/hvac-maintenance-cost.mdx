# HVAC Maintenance Cost

Regular maintenance is the cheapest thing you can do for a heating and cooling system. ENERGY STAR recommends a pre-season checkup by a contractor, the cooling system in spring and the heating system in fall, plus filter changes you handle yourself.

**A standard tune-up typically costs about $75 to $150 for a central air conditioner and $80 to $160 for a gas furnace, or $155 to $310 a year for both. Heat pumps and mini splits run a little more because they're serviced for both seasons.** These are typical ranges from contractor pricing; local rates vary.

## Tune-up costs by system

| System | Per visit | Per year |
|---|---|---|
| Central air conditioner | $75 to $150 | $75 to $150 |
| Gas furnace | $80 to $160 | $80 to $160 |
| Air conditioner and gas furnace | $75 to $160 each | $155 to $310 |
| Air-source heat pump | $100 to $175 | $200 to $350 (two visits) |
| Ductless mini split | $80 to $150 per indoor head | $160 to $300 per head (two visits) |
| Boiler | $100 to $200 | $100 to $200 |
| Oil furnace | $150 to $250 | $150 to $250 |
| Geothermal heat pump | $125 to $225 | $250 to $450 (two visits) |

After-hours and emergency calls typically cost more. Repairs found during a tune-up, such as a capacitor or refrigerant leak, are extra.

## What drives the price in your area

The spread in each range above is mostly labor. A tune-up is an hour or two of a technician's time, so the price tracks local labor rates and cost of living more than the equipment itself.

Higher-cost metros and much of the Northeast sit toward the top of each range, while the South and rural areas tend toward the bottom. A long drive to your home can add a trip charge, and nights, weekends and peak-season calls usually carry a premium.

Two visits cost more than one for the same reason, not because the second trip uses more parts. You are paying again for the technician's time and travel.

## Do the savings cover the tune-up?

Maintenance pays for itself mainly by keeping the system efficient and heading off breakdowns. The clearest figure is the filter: the DOE says a clean filter can lower an air conditioner's energy use by 5 to 15%, and coil cleaning and a correct refrigerant charge protect that same efficiency.

Put that against a running cost. A 3-ton air conditioner at the federal-minimum 14.3 SEER2, run 1,500 full-load hours a year at 18 cents per kWh, uses 3 × 12,000 × 1,500 ÷ (14.3 × 1,000), which is about 3,776 kWh, or roughly $680 a year to cool. The hours are an example; a shorter northern season uses fewer and a long southern one more.

A 5 to 15% efficiency loss on that $680 works out to about $34 to $102 a year. At the top of that band it can approach the $75 to $150 cost of a single air-conditioner tune-up, before counting the repairs a visit catches early, such as a clogged condensate drain or a weak capacitor.

## What a tune-up should include

ENERGY STAR's checklist for a contractor visit includes:

- **Thermostat:** check settings and operation.
- **Electrical:** tighten connections and measure voltage and current on motors.
- **Moving parts:** lubricate where needed; check belts.
- **Condensate drain:** clear it, so it doesn't overflow or grow mold.
- **Controls:** check that the system starts, runs and shuts off properly.
- **Cooling:** clean the evaporator and condenser coils and check the refrigerant level.
- **Heating:** check gas or oil connections, gas pressure, burner combustion and the heat exchanger.

A visit that skips most of this isn't a full tune-up. Ask what's included before booking.

## Service plans vs. paying per visit

Many contractors sell annual plans that bundle two tune-ups with perks such as priority scheduling or waived diagnostic fees. Compare the plan's yearly price with two individual tune-ups from the table above, and read what it actually covers; a plan pays off mainly if you'd use its repair discounts or priority service.

The break-even is easy to pin down. Two separate tune-ups for an air conditioner and a gas furnace run about $155 to $310 a year combined, from the table above. A plan priced below that band is cheaper than paying per visit even before any perks, while one priced above it only pays off if its repair discounts, waived fees or priority service close the gap.

## A yearly maintenance budget

For a typical home with central air conditioning and a gas furnace, the recurring cost is two pre-season tune-ups a year plus the filter changes you handle yourself. From the table above, the two tune-ups run about $155 to $310 a year combined.

Filters are the smallest line in that budget, so the two tune-ups set the yearly figure. A heat pump home runs higher, closer to $200 to $350 a year, because the same unit is serviced in both the heating and cooling seasons.

## Maintenance you can do yourself

- **Change or clean the filter** as the system's maker recommends. The DOE says clean filters can lower an air conditioner's energy use by 5 to 15%.
- **Keep the outdoor unit clear** of leaves, grass and debris, with a couple of feet of open space around it.
- **Keep supply and return vents unblocked.**
- **Watch the condensate line** for clogs during cooling season.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does an HVAC tune-up cost?

Typically $75 to $150 for an air conditioner and $80 to $160 for a gas furnace per visit; local rates vary.

### How often should HVAC be serviced?

ENERGY STAR recommends a pre-season checkup: the cooling system in spring and the heating system in fall. Heat pumps, which do both, get both.

### Is an HVAC maintenance plan worth it?

If it costs less than two individual tune-ups and you'd use its perks, often yes. Compare the yearly price with paying per visit.

### What does an AC tune-up include?

Coil cleaning, refrigerant and electrical checks, clearing the condensate drain, lubricating moving parts and testing the controls and thermostat.

### Is a $99 HVAC tune-up legit?

A $99 price sits inside the typical $75 to $150 air-conditioner range, so it can be a real tune-up. The risk is a stripped visit that skips coil cleaning or the refrigerant and electrical checks, so ask what is included and be cautious with a very cheap call that then produces a long repair list.

### How much does it cost to recharge refrigerant?

A recharge price depends on the refrigerant type and how much the system lost, and a correctly sealed system should not need topping up. Needing refrigerant means there is a leak, so the real job is finding and fixing that leak; adding refrigerant without it just delays the same failure.

### Why are heat pump tune-ups more expensive?

A heat pump both heats and cools through one refrigerant circuit, so it is checked in both the cooling and heating seasons rather than once a year. That means two visits, which is why its annual total of about $200 to $350 runs above a single air-conditioner or furnace tune-up.
