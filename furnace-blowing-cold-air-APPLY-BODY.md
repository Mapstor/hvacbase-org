# Furnace Blowing Cold Air?

A furnace that blows cold air is usually telling you one of a few things: it hasn't warmed up yet, its fan is set to run all the time, or a safety switch has shut the burner off while the blower keeps going. Most causes are quick to check.

**Check the thermostat first: if the fan is set to On instead of Auto, the blower runs and pushes unheated air between cycles. Next check the filter; a clogged filter can overheat the furnace, which shuts the burner off while the fan keeps running. If the burner won't stay lit, the flame sensor, ignition or, on a high-efficiency furnace, a clogged condensate drain is the usual suspect.**

## Quick checks

- **Thermostat set to Heat**, with the setpoint above room temperature.
- **Fan set to Auto, not On.** On runs the blower even when the burner is off.
- **Give it a few minutes.** Many furnaces run the burner briefly before the blower starts, and run the blower after the burner stops to use the remaining heat.
- **The filter.** Replace it if it's dirty; the DOE recommends checking furnace filters monthly or as the maker recommends.

## Overheating and the limit switch

When airflow is restricted, by a clogged filter, closed vents or blocked returns, the heat exchanger gets too hot. A high-limit switch then shuts the burner off to protect it, while the blower keeps running to cool the furnace, so you feel cool air.

Fix the airflow; repeated overheating stresses the heat exchanger. See [cracked heat exchanger](/cracked-heat-exchanger).

## Burner won't light or stay lit

- **Dirty flame sensor:** the furnace lights, then shuts the gas off after a few seconds because it can't confirm the flame.
- **Ignition problems:** a failed hot-surface igniter, or an older pilot that's out.
- **Gas supply:** a closed valve or a supply problem.
- **Clogged condensate drain** on a high-efficiency furnace, which trips a safety switch; see [furnace leaking water](/furnace-leaking-water).

Many furnaces flash a diagnostic code on the control board, explained on the label inside the door.

## Cold air at some registers only

Leaky or disconnected ducts in an attic or crawl space can deliver cool air to some rooms even when the furnace heats normally. Check the duct runs to those rooms.

## If you have a heat pump

A heat pump's air feels cooler than a furnace's, and during a defrost cycle it can blow cool air for a few minutes. That's normal; see [heat pumps in cold weather](/heat-pump-in-cold-weather).

## When to call a technician

- The burner won't light, or lights and goes out repeatedly after you've checked the filter.
- You smell gas: leave the house and call your gas utility from outside.
- A carbon monoxide alarm sounds.
- The limit switch keeps tripping even with a clean filter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my furnace blowing cold air?

Most often the thermostat fan is set to On, the furnace hasn't warmed up yet, or a clogged filter has made it overheat and shut off the burner.

### Can a dirty filter make a furnace blow cold air?

Yes. Restricted airflow can overheat the furnace, and a safety switch shuts the burner off while the blower keeps running.

### Why does my furnace blow cold air at the start?

Some furnaces start the blower before the heat exchanger is fully warm, or after the burner shuts off; a short burst of cool air is normal.

### Should I turn off my furnace if it's blowing cold air?

If the burner won't stay lit or a limit switch keeps tripping, turn it off and call a technician. If you smell gas, leave and call your gas utility.
