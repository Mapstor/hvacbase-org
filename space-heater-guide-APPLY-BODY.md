# Space Heater Guide

A space heater warms one room without heating the whole house, which makes it useful for a cold office, a basement or a drafty bedroom. Choosing one is mostly about the room, how you'll use it, and safety, because every electric model turns electricity into heat at the same rate.

**Most plug-in electric space heaters top out at 1,500 watts, about 5,100 BTU per hour, and cost about 27 cents an hour at 18 cents per kWh. All electric resistance types are equally efficient; choose by how they deliver heat, and look for safety certification, tip-over shutoff and overheat protection.**

## Types of electric space heaters

- **Convection, including oil-filled radiators:** warm the air in the room slowly and evenly, quiet, and hold heat after cycling off.
- **Fan-forced and ceramic:** blow heated air for fast warm-up, with some fan noise.
- **Radiant or infrared:** heat people and objects in front of them directly, good for spot heating in drafty spaces.

Each turns essentially all its electricity into heat; the difference is comfort and speed, not cost per hour at the same wattage.

## Sizing

A 1,500-watt heater delivers about 5,100 BTU per hour (1,500 × 3.412). That suits a small to medium, reasonably insulated room as supplemental heat. A cold, drafty or large room may need more than one heater can give, which points to a permanent solution such as a ductless heat pump; see [how many mini splits you need](/how-many-mini-splits-do-i-need).

## Running cost

Cost = watts × hours ÷ 1,000 × your rate: a 1,500-watt heater costs about 27 cents an hour at 18 cents per kWh, about $65 a month at 8 hours a day. See [electric heater running cost](/electric-heater-running-cost) for more examples and a calculator.

## Safety features to look for

- **Certification** by a nationally recognized testing laboratory.
- **Tip-over shutoff**, which turns the heater off if it's knocked over.
- **Overheat protection**, which shuts it off if internal parts get too hot.
- **A thermostat**, so it cycles instead of running at full power.

## Using a space heater safely

- **Plug it directly into a wall outlet**, not an extension cord or power strip; a 1,500-watt heater draws 12.5 amps.
- **Keep it at least 3 feet** from bedding, curtains, furniture and anything else that can burn.
- **Place it on a hard, level floor**, away from foot traffic.
- **Turn it off** when you leave the room or go to sleep, unless it's designed for unattended use.

## Gas and kerosene heaters

Fuel-burning heaters that aren't vented outdoors release combustion gases, including carbon monoxide, into the room. If you use one, follow its instructions carefully, ventilate as directed and keep CO alarms working; see the [carbon monoxide detector guide](/carbon-monoxide-detector-guide).

## When a heat pump makes more sense

The DOE notes that heat pumps can cut electricity used for heating by about half compared with electric resistance heat. For a room you heat every day, a ductless heat pump's lower running cost usually outweighs its higher price.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the most efficient space heater?

All electric resistance space heaters are equally efficient at turning electricity into heat. Choose by how they deliver heat and by safety features.

### How much does it cost to run a space heater?

About 27 cents an hour for a 1,500-watt heater at 18 cents per kWh.

### Can a space heater heat a whole room?

A small to medium, insulated room, yes, as supplemental heat. Large or drafty rooms may need more than one heater can deliver.

### Is it safe to leave a space heater on overnight?

Only if it's designed for unattended use, has tip-over and overheat shutoff, and is at least 3 feet from anything that can burn. Otherwise, turn it off.
