# Do Portable ACs Pull Air From Outside?

A portable air conditioner doesn't have a fresh-air intake, but a single-hose model ends up drawing outdoor air into your home anyway. Where that air comes from, and what it brings with it, depends on the hose design.

**A single-hose portable blows room air outdoors to cool its condenser, which leaves the room at slightly lower pressure, so warm outdoor air leaks in through gaps around doors and windows. A dual-hose portable takes its condenser air from outside through a second hose and returns it outside, so it pulls in much less. Neither type ventilates the room with filtered fresh air.**

## How a single-hose portable pulls air in

Every cubic foot of room air the single-hose unit exhausts has to be replaced. It comes back in through the easiest path: gaps around windows and doors, other rooms, the attic or crawl space. That replacement air is unconditioned, so part of the unit's cooling goes into cooling outdoor air, and it's unfiltered.

## What dual-hose units do differently

A dual-hose unit draws outdoor air through an intake hose, passes it over the condenser and sends it back out the exhaust hose. The room air stays in its loop across the cold coil, so the room isn't depressurized. The federal test that rates portables accounts for this difference; see [single-hose vs. dual-hose portable ACs](/single-hose-vs-dual-hose-portable-ac).

## Do portables bring in fresh air?

Not in a useful way. A single-hose unit pulls in outdoor air, but uncontrolled and unfiltered, through whatever gaps exist. A dual-hose unit keeps the outdoor air in its own circuit.

For actual ventilation, use a window, an exhaust fan or a ventilation system; see [how to improve indoor air quality](/how-to-improve-indoor-air-quality).

## Wildfire smoke

During smoke events, the EPA's advice is to keep smoke out and clean the air in a room you can close up, with a filter or air cleaner that captures fine particles. A single-hose portable works against that by drawing outdoor air in through gaps. A dual-hose unit, a sealed window kit and a portable HEPA air cleaner in the same room keep the air cleaner.

## Reducing outdoor air leakage

- **Seal the window kit** and the gaps around it with foam or tape.
- **Keep the exhaust hose short and straight.**
- **Close the room's door** so the unit cools one space.
- **Consider a dual-hose model**, or a window unit if your window allows it; see [portable vs. window AC](/portable-vs-window-ac).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a portable AC bring in fresh air?

Not usefully. A single-hose unit pulls outdoor air in through gaps as it exhausts room air, but uncontrolled and unfiltered; a dual-hose unit keeps outdoor air in its own circuit.

### Do single-hose portable ACs create negative pressure?

Yes. They exhaust room air outdoors, so outdoor air leaks in to replace it.

### Are dual-hose portable ACs better?

For efficiency and keeping outdoor air out, usually yes, because they don't depressurize the room.

### Can I use a portable AC during wildfire smoke?

Yes, but a single-hose unit will pull some smoky air in through gaps. Seal the window kit, close up the room, and run a HEPA air cleaner, as the EPA's clean-room guidance suggests.
